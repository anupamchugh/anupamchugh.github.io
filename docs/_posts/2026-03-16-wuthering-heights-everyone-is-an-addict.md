Watched Emerald Fennell's Wuthering Heights (2026). The standard read is doomed romance — Heathcliff and Catherine can't be together, everyone suffers. But what struck me is simpler: every main character is an addict. Just to different things.

**Hindley** — gambling and alcohol. Numbs himself quietly, drinks and gambles the estate into ruin. The slow, invisible kind of self-destruction.

**Heathcliff** — addicted to Catherine, then to revenge when he can't have her. Marries Isabella not out of love but to hurt Catherine. The obsession never changes object — it just changes shape.

**Catherine** — addicted to wanting. She needs Heathcliff's wildness and Edgar's comfort. Not one or the other — both. When she loses control of the arrangement, she breaks. Dies of septicemia after losing the baby.

**Isabella** — addicted to the idea of Heathcliff. Submits to his cruelty willingly, chasing a version of him that never existed.

**Nelly** — the quiet one. Addicted to proximity to drama. Stirs the pot from the shadows while appearing loyal.

The father numbs. The hero fixates. The heroine grasps. And their dysfunction radiates outward to contaminate everyone around them.

It's not a love story. It's a study in what happens when nobody can stop.
