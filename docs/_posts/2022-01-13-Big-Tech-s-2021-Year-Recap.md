When 2021 started, we all looked forward to a calmer year with hopes of
a return to normalcy. But it didn’t pan out the way we all expected with
the gruesome second wave during March and April followed by an Omicron
scare at the end of the year.

Although, from the perspective of Big Tech, the year was off to an
intense start — and that’s excluding the anti-trust investigations
they’ve been facing lately. The Parler app got booted off the internet
by Amazon, Google, and Apple — which (without getting into the morality
debate) was perhaps a huge signal that Big Tech has gotten too powerful.

From regulatory troubles to metaverse and space travels to brief outages
and failed experiments, so much happened in the Big Tech during 2021,
that it’s difficult to keep up. Yet, there are quite a few similar bets
made by Meta, Google, Apple, Amazon, and Microsoft (or `MAGMA`, if we
call it so now)

Each of the firms has taken long strides either to leverage from the
content-creator economy, increase their advertising market share, or
infiltrate healthcare. To top of it all, all of them are working on
their own mixed reality ecosystem and a passwordless future (okay, maybe
Amazon hasn’t jumped in yet).

Below, we’ll do a roundup of the major moves made by Meta, Google, and
Apple— how they fared — success and disappointments — and what might lie
transpire during 2022.

### Meta — Zuck’s Escape Plan

Facebook (now known as Meta) had an eventful 2021. After criticizing
Apple for introducing limited ad-tracking in iOS 14, Facebook launched a
bazooka in the form of WhatsApp.

“<a
href="https://medium.com/big-tech/facebooks-latest-ploy-against-apple-my-way-or-the-highway-4f5b0e2d37b7"
class="markup--anchor markup--p-anchor"
data-href="https://medium.com/big-tech/facebooks-latest-ploy-against-apple-my-way-or-the-highway-4f5b0e2d37b7"
target="_blank">Send your WhatsApp data or lose the account</a>” was at
the heart of their latest privacy policy update for iPhone users. Much
to their dismay, the move didn’t reap dividends. As users threatened to
switch to more privacy-preserved messengers, Facebook backtracked their
policy — thereby making the whole campaign laughable.

But it didn’t stop them from pushing the boundaries with Apple’s App
Store rules. Once, by <a
href="https://medium.com/big-tech/facebook-is-gaslighting-users-into-allowing-ad-tracking-on-ios-14-7df7dd3f7359"
class="markup--anchor markup--p-anchor"
data-href="https://medium.com/big-tech/facebook-is-gaslighting-users-into-allowing-ad-tracking-on-ios-14-7df7dd3f7359"
target="_blank">gaslighting iPhone users</a> into believing personalized
ads are good. And then by <a
href="https://www.theverge.com/2021/11/3/22761620/facebook-apple-app-store-fees-subscription-links-creators"
class="markup--anchor markup--p-anchor"
data-href="https://www.theverge.com/2021/11/3/22761620/facebook-apple-app-store-fees-subscription-links-creators"
rel="noopener" target="_blank">skirting Apple’s App Store fees with
custom subscription links</a>.

Well, their ad wars with Apple didn’t last for long on the front pages,
as Facebook found itself in the midst of the biggest scandal since
inception: “Facebook Papers”.

The <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Facebook_leak"
class="markup--anchor markup--p-anchor"
data-href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Facebook_leak"
rel="noopener" target="_blank">internal documents leaked</a> by
whistleblower <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frances_Haugen"
class="markup--anchor markup--p-anchor"
data-href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frances_Haugen" rel="noopener"
target="_blank">Frances Haugen</a> offered revelations that led the Wall
Street Journal to do a thorough investigation. *The Facebook Files*
produced by WSJ revealed how Facebook knows Instagram is causing mental
health issues among teenagers, their algorithm incentivizes anger
reactionary posts, and how they still fail to fix the flaws.

A global outage across Facebook’s family apps only further deepened the
crisis the company was facing. *The remedy?* We saw Facebook rebranding
and morphing into Meta — Zuckerberg’s big gamble on Metaverse.

While it’s still too early to form an opinion on the Meta play, it seems
highly likely that Zuck’s vision is to build the infrastructure for the
metaverse and minimize Facebook’s reliance on Google and Apple’s mobile
ecosystem. Additionally, Meta also seems to be an attempt to keep
Instagram and WhatsApp away from the PR nightmare that Facebook is going
through.

### Google — Familiar Woes

One of the most awaited releases from Google in 2021 was their
replacement tool for cookie tracking in the Chrome browser.

For starters, Google had already planned to phase out third-party
cookies. So, when Google announced a new approach for ad-tracking,
privacy-first web, it did cause a stir — more so, since the tech giant
stood to benefit most from their new FLoC technology — as it gets to
live on the Chrome browser only.

To add more fuel to the fire, FLoC doesn’t really stop tracking.
Instead, it just aggregates into groups (or cohorts) based on
interests.No surprise, Google’s FLoC ad system was unanimously blocked
by Apple, Amazon, GitHub, Firefox, and others.

Eventually, Google <a
href="https://arstechnica.com/gadgets/2021/06/google-delays-floc-rollout-until-2023/"
class="markup--anchor markup--p-anchor"
data-href="https://arstechnica.com/gadgets/2021/06/google-delays-floc-rollout-until-2023/"
rel="noopener" target="_blank">delayed FLoC</a> until it finds a
solution that’s lauded by both privacy advocates and marketers. However,
if history is anything to go by, FLoC as we know it will probably
feature in Google’s graveyard of services.

Besides, FLoC wasn’t the only service that didn’t see light at the end
of the tunnel. Google’s much-hyped Stadia project brought some
disappointing news. Stadia Games and Entertainment, Google’s in-house
division for developing games was sunsetted. Perhaps, after the lukewarm
response, their cloud gaming service got, Google only plans to use <a
href="https://arstechnica.com/gadgets/2021/10/google-stadias-salvaged-future-as-a-back-end-cloud-service-is-here/"
class="markup--anchor markup--p-anchor"
data-href="https://arstechnica.com/gadgets/2021/10/google-stadias-salvaged-future-as-a-back-end-cloud-service-is-here/"
rel="noopener" target="_blank">Stadia as a platform to power third-party
games</a>.

While Facebook and Twitter kept driving their newsletters business
models, Google pulled the curtains on its nascent newsletter platform
called <a href="https://museletter.area120.google.com/"
class="markup--anchor markup--p-anchor"
data-href="https://museletter.area120.google.com/" rel="noopener"
target="_blank">Museletter</a>.

Okay, Google’s 2021 wasn’t just about failed experiments. They did
advance with their <a
href="https://blog.google/technology/research/2021-year-review-google-quantum-ai/"
class="markup--anchor markup--p-anchor"
data-href="https://blog.google/technology/research/2021-year-review-google-quantum-ai/"
rel="noopener" target="_blank">Quantum AI</a> vision, made Android 12
more privacy-focused, and re-introduced widgets with more pleasing
aesthetics. My personal favorite Google feature was the introduction of
Lens on Desktop Chrome.

There are also murmurs about Google working on its AR/VR headset
(again?).

### Apple — Misfired Shots

Apple always has a knack for being in the news — even when they’d wish
not to — and 2021 was no different. Much of the first half of Apple's
year was spent fighting a two-front war with Epic Games and Facebook.

For those who don’t know, Apple and the Fortnite game maker were caught
in a year-long feud over the App Store fees after the latter bypassed
Apple Payments which took the battle to the court. By Oct 2021, the
contentious legal battle did get some closure — with the judge ruling it
in Apple’s favor on most fronts.

Although, Apple’s win was diminished when the judge forced them to allow
external payment options (and prompts) in the App Store. Despite best
attempts, Apple only managed to delay third-party payment features in a
last-minute plea — leaving a gaping hole in their App Store. As
<a href="https://medium.com/@mgs"
class="markup--anchor markup--p-anchor"
data-href="https://medium.com/@mgs" target="_blank">M.G. Siegler</a>
summed it up in his post: “<a
href="https://500ish.com/apple-won-a-battle-to-lose-the-war-3ce6c3701918"
class="markup--anchor markup--p-anchor"
data-href="https://500ish.com/apple-won-a-battle-to-lose-the-war-3ce6c3701918"
rel="noopener" target="_blank">Apple won a Battle to Lose the War</a>”.

We did see the vintage Apple though during WWDC 2021, “Unleashed” and
“Spring Loaded” events. <a
href="https://medium.com/big-tech/ios-15-hands-on-its-the-little-things-that-make-a-big-difference-ee858c2e76e4"
class="markup--anchor markup--p-anchor"
data-href="https://medium.com/big-tech/ios-15-hands-on-its-the-little-things-that-make-a-big-difference-ee858c2e76e4"
target="_blank">Handy iOS 15 features</a> (some of which might actually
persuade you to <a
href="https://medium.com/big-tech/with-ios-15-apple-gave-us-strong-reasons-to-ditch-google-services-b43b369d6be9"
class="markup--anchor markup--p-anchor"
data-href="https://medium.com/big-tech/with-ios-15-apple-gave-us-strong-reasons-to-ditch-google-services-b43b369d6be9"
target="_blank">ditch Google apps entirely</a>), a new iPad mini, Apple
Cards, and enhancements to other services were praised by the tech
community. However, it was the M1 Pro and M1 max that became the show
stealers of the year. With a 16-core GPU and a much longer battery life,
the new Macbook Pro is truly a performance beast — and miles ahead of
its predecessors.

In spite of the highs that Apple made with its product upgrades in 2021,
two of their promising feature turned out as duds.

Much had already been talked and propped about App Tracking
Transparency. It threatened to <a
href="https://medium.com/macoclock/apple-is-killing-a-billion-dollar-ad-industry-with-one-popup-2f83d182837f"
class="markup--anchor markup--p-anchor"
data-href="https://medium.com/macoclock/apple-is-killing-a-billion-dollar-ad-industry-with-one-popup-2f83d182837f"
target="_blank">end the era of personalized ads</a> — which is why it
got flak from Facebook and the other ad networks. After much delay, when
App Tracking Transparency finally saw the light of the day, it didn’t
really live up to the promise.

<a
href="https://www.washingtonpost.com/technology/2021/09/23/iphone-tracking/"
class="markup--anchor markup--p-anchor"
data-href="https://www.washingtonpost.com/technology/2021/09/23/iphone-tracking/"
rel="noopener" target="_blank">Washington Post’s analysts found out</a>
that apps were able to workaround ATT through fingerprinting (using
user’s device-specific data like IP address, battery level to build a
unique profile). As the lines between fingerprinting and ad-tracking are
slightly blurred, there wasn’t much Apple could do to restrict
probabilistic matching immediately — making App Tracking Transparency a
letdown for the users. It was still a success for the iPhone Maker
though, as <a
href="https://www.forbes.com/sites/johnkoetsier/2021/10/19/apples-ad-network-is-the-biggest-beneficiary-of-apples-new-marketing-rules-report/?sh=5b8094c477a0"
class="markup--anchor markup--p-anchor"
data-href="https://www.forbes.com/sites/johnkoetsier/2021/10/19/apples-ad-network-is-the-biggest-beneficiary-of-apples-new-marketing-rules-report/?sh=5b8094c477a0"
rel="noopener" target="_blank">Apple Search Ads revenue saw a surge in
market share</a>.

More than App Tracking Transparency, Apple’s biggest disappointment of
the year was the CSAM scanning technology. Deemed as a privacy feature
for child safety, it was pushing the boundaries of what censorship
means. After getting critiqued by security experts and fearing more
backlash from consumers, Apple has put CSAM on the back burner.

2021 turned out to be a strange year for Apple. They almost touched a
\$3T market cap making them the most valuable technology company in the
world. At the same time, Apple is looking more vulnerable than ever with
the App Store payment’s future hanging by a thread. The fear of losing
its App Store revenue share to external payments has already led <a
href="https://www.forbes.com/sites/johnkoetsier/2021/11/12/apple-quietly-buying-ads-via-google-for-high-value-subscription-apps-to-capture-app-publisher-revenue/?sh=556f89f11b52"
class="markup--anchor markup--p-anchor"
data-href="https://www.forbes.com/sites/johnkoetsier/2021/11/12/apple-quietly-buying-ads-via-google-for-high-value-subscription-apps-to-capture-app-publisher-revenue/?sh=556f89f11b52"
rel="noopener" target="_blank">Apple to buy more Google ads</a> — in
order to redirect web users to the store.

Alongside external payment features, the debacle of App Tracking
Transparency might give rise to more super apps as every publisher would
look to build their own ecosystem and harness first-party data. Or as
Scott Galloway says: “<a
href="https://marker.medium.com/super-apps-are-the-future-2f4884ad2266"
class="markup--anchor markup--p-anchor"
data-href="https://marker.medium.com/super-apps-are-the-future-2f4884ad2266"
rel="noopener" target="_blank">Super apps are inevitable</a>”. Netflix
has already started offering in-app games — which though redirects us to
the App Store at the moment — might spell trouble for the tech giant in
the future.

Amazon had a rather low-key year in comparison to the above three.
Barring an AWS outage, Bezos’s farewell, <a
href="https://www.businessinsider.com/amazon-insiders-already-worry-astro-home-robot-will-fail-2021-9?IR=T"
class="markup--anchor markup--p-anchor"

conditions, not a lot of news got the limelight. By the way `Alexa.com`
is sunsetting in May 2022. *No, not the one you thought*.

From Microsoft’s perspective, if one lets the YK22 email bug and the
persistent Windows 11 issues slide, they’ve had a rather
controversy-free year when compared to the other Big Tech firms. Windows
11's support for Android apps could bring some much-needed popularity to
Microsoft’s app store. But a failed acquisition of the popular social
media company, Discord might prove to be a small setback for the
software giant’s social gaming business.
