What if looking at the market felt more like navigating a world than reading a wall of numbers?

TradeShot Space is an exploration of that question. One direction maps symbols around a benchmark so that their positions can communicate relative strength. Another development branch experiments with a spaceship and market targets using RealityKit.

The spaceship is an idea in development, not a released trading game. I haven't captured or verified a complete game session for this post. The [showcase](/#glint) introduces it as an exploration, alongside Glint and the broader TradeShot practice idea.

## A gym for trading

I think of Glint and TradeShot as customizable tools for building better trading habits: practice, steps, runs and reps. A game could make a session more engaging, but the game mechanic has to help someone see or rehearse something useful. Flying around a beautiful chart isn't enough on its own.

A possible exercise might ask a player to notice which symbols are gaining strength relative to a benchmark, then revisit what happened in a recorded session. That is a design idea, not a demonstrated feature. The key question is whether the visual helps a person understand the underlying information.

## Keep practice and the brokerage separate

A simulated target, a recorded market, and a live quote are different things. The interface should say which one it is showing. A game should not quietly turn a practice action into a brokerage order.

The TradeShot Space project describes its market view as read-only, with recorded sessions labelled when live data isn't available. That is a useful boundary for an experiment. Before showing the spaceship as a working product, I want a real capture, clear data labels, and a chance to test whether the interaction teaches anything.

There is room to make market tools more playful. There is also room to make the rules of the game much clearer than the rules we sometimes invent for ourselves while trading.
